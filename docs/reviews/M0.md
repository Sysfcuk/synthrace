# M0 review: technical spikes and pipeline

**Status:** deployed at https://sysfcuk.github.io/synthrace/ (repo: https://github.com/Sysfcuk/synthrace). The Pages pipeline is green, including the live-site Playwright check.

## What was built

- **Workspace:** a Cargo workspace with five crates:
  - `render`: the wgpu renderer and WGSL shaders.
  - `dsp`: the real-time synth.
  - `dsp-worklet`: the AudioWorklet ABI, built as a no-bindgen cdylib.
  - `game`: the wasm entry point, with input, audio bridge and debug overlay.
  - `desktop`: a headless native runner for screenshots and benchmarks.
- **Render spike** ("Neon Bent-World"):
  - Ground lattice in track space, bent in the vertex shader by an accumulated curve/hill profile, plus horizon curvature.
  - Ridged-fBm wireframe mountains, drawn with anti-aliased `fwidth` grid lines.
  - Road with emissive lane, edge and rumble markings.
  - Analytic banded sun, star field, and fog that matches the sky colour.
  - RGBA16F HDR target, then a 6-level COD:AW bloom chain (13-tap downsample with a Karis average, tent-filter upsample; no blending needed), then an AgX "punchy" composite with radial speed blur, chromatic aberration, vignette and grain.
  - 14 draw calls in 13 passes.
- **Backend selection:** the browser uses WebGPU when available, through `new_instance_with_webgpu_detection`. `?backend=gl` forces WebGL2. It is a single binary, built to the WebGL2 downlevel limits.
- **Audio spike:**
  - A Rust synth with PolyBLEP supersaw pads, octave bass, kick with sidechain pump, off-beat hats and an arpeggio, playing an Am–F–C–G progression at 118 BPM.
  - It runs in an AudioWorklet. The DSP wasm bytes are embedded in the game wasm and handed to the worklet through `processorOptions`, and the worklet shim is loaded from a Blob URL. No SharedArrayBuffer, threads or COOP/COEP are needed.
  - Speed is sent with `postMessage` and opens the filter and brings in layers: drums above 15% speed, arpeggio above 60%.
  - The worklet reports its beat clock back, and the main thread uses it to drive beat-synced pulses in the sun and grid.
  - Audio unlocks on a user gesture through PRESS START. `navigator.audioSession` is set to "playback" for iOS, the context suspends and resumes on visibility changes, and M toggles mute.
- **Pipeline:**
  - trunk configuration with a pre-build hook that compiles the DSP.
  - `ci.yml`: fmt, clippy (native and wasm), tests, web build, size budget.
  - `pages.yml`: build, then `upload-pages-artifact@v5` and `deploy-pages@v5`, then a verify job (HTTP and MIME checks plus a Playwright smoke test on the live URL).

## Findings and decisions

1. **wgpu with WebGPU and WebGL2 in one binary is viable.**
   - It comes to about 1 MB gzip with the renderer included, far below the 2.5 MB budget.
   - The GL fallback renders the same image: compare `native-gles-curve.png` with `native-vulkan-curve.png`, and the browser screenshots.
   - **Recommendation: proceed with wgpu.**
2. **naga is more permissive than Chrome's WGSL compiler (Tint).**
   - `a * b ^ c` passed naga but Tint rejected it, because WGSL requires parentheses when mixing `*` and `^`.
   - The native tests did not catch it; the browser did. **Lesson: browser runs are mandatory** (now enforced by the Playwright smoke test in the deploy pipeline).
   - Follow-up: a browser-side shader compile check for all modules, using `getCompilationInfo`, as part of the e2e suite in M1.
3. **wasm-opt needs explicit feature flags.**
   - Rust 1.100 emits bulk-memory and other post-MVP wasm features. wasm-opt version_131 rejects the module unless those features are enabled.
   - Fixed through `data-wasm-opt-params` in `web/index.html`.
4. **The AudioWorklet pattern works:** a no-bindgen Rust cdylib, zero imports, synchronous instantiation inside the worklet, about 1% of one CPU core. **Recommendation: proceed.**
5. **HDR is available on both paths.** RGBA16F is renderable and filterable on browser WebGPU and on WebGL2 through ANGLE.
6. **Surface formats differ by backend.** WebGPU gives `Bgra8Unorm`, so the shader applies the sRGB encode manually. WebGL2 gives `Rgba8UnormSrgb`. Both paths produce the same look.

## Known weaknesses

These are spike-quality and not final.

- **Art:**
  - The grade is soft and low-contrast, and the road reads too purple.
  - The sun loses its magenta gradient under AgX.
  - The ground grid near the horizon gets busy, despite the anti-moiré fade.
  - All of this is tuned in the M1 art pass.
- **No car.** Steering only slides the camera, and the driving model is a placeholder. The real handling arrives in M1.
- **Bloom:** the smallest bloom "up" texture is allocated but unused (a minor waste).
- **Audio musicality:** reviewed only through automated metrics so far. **A human listening check is needed.**
- **Mobile:** no mobile device tested yet (touch input exists but is unverified).
