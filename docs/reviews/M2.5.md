# M2.5 review: handling rework and knockable traffic

These changes respond to the user's playtest notes: "it glides and doesn't act like a car" and "the other cars seem immovable."
The research and design behind them are in [../research/HANDLING.md](../research/HANDLING.md).

## What changed

### Vehicle model (`sim::car`)

The old model moved the car sideways directly with steering, which is why it felt like it glided. It is replaced by a velocity-alignment ("bicycle-lite") model:

- **Heading and travel direction.** The car has a body heading and a separate direction of travel. Tyre grip pulls the travel direction toward the heading.
- **Steering demands a yaw rate.** The rate is capped by steering geometry at low speed and by grip (μ = 1.4) at high speed, so full steer at 300 km/h means "use all the grip".
- **The body has yaw inertia,** so turn-in and unwind take a moment.
- **The road curves underneath the car.** Without steering you run wide in bends. This replaces the old scripted centrifugal push.
- **Sideways motion has momentum.** Let go of the steering and you keep moving until you countersteer. A mild recentre assist keeps keyboard play manageable.
- **Cornering and sliding scrub speed.** Off-road, grip drops to μ = 0.8.

### Drift

- The car holds a **target slip angle**: steering into the drift widens it, countersteer narrows it to zero.
- The path carves at the grip limit, and countersteer turns the path out of the drift.
- The drift ends once you've countersteered it straight.
- Tiers charge while the car is actually sliding, at double speed when the angle is tight.
- **Brake-tap drift now requires staying on the throttle.** Ordinary braking never starts a drift.

### Deterministic trigonometry (`sim::dmath`)

- Polynomial sin, cos, atan and atan2, built only from IEEE-exact operations.
- The new golden hash is `0x6c6d4586e25017f0`, and the wasm build reproduces it exactly (verified in the browser).
- The determinism probe is now closed-loop: the autopilot drives, with scripted drifts, nitro bursts and brake pulses on top, so all of those systems are covered.

### Collisions are momentum exchanges

- Each car has a mass: player 1300 kg, coupe 1300, sports 1250, van 2100, truck 9000.
- Impacts transfer momentum with restitution 0.25, along the axis of least penetration.
- Against light traffic, the player counts as 2600 kg (Burnout-style "traffic checking"). Ramming those cars launches them forward instead of crashing you. Rear-ending a truck hard spins you out.
- **Wrecked traffic** has its AI switched off. It slides under friction with spin, smashes into palms, and causes pile-ups (wreck-to-traffic impulses, chained).
- **Takedowns:** a car you wreck that leaves the road, hits scenery, or hits other traffic awards +15 nitro, a TAKEDOWN! callout, a sound, a screen flash and a short hit-stop.

### Visual weight (render only)

- The car body sits on a sprung, slightly underdamped suspension. It rolls about 4°/g out of corners, squats under acceleration and dives under braking.
- The body yaws to its real heading, so drift angles are visible.
- The camera yaws 35% with the car's heading.
- Wrecked traffic spins visibly and is interpolated laterally.
- Pile-ups throw sparks and make impact sounds that fade with distance.

### Autopilot (attract mode)

- Rewritten for the new model: heading control, time-to-collision lane choice, and full braking.
- It creeps round stopped wrecks. Before this fix, it could get stuck forever behind one.

## Tests

50 native tests in total:
- `sim` 34, including new handling and collision tests:
  - momentum and no glide;
  - grip-limited yaw and speed scrub;
  - running wide in a bend;
  - drift slide and countersteer exit;
  - traffic checking;
  - truck spin;
  - sideswipe takedown;
  - pile-ups;
- `dsp` 7, `procgen` 4, `game` 4, `render` 1.

Clippy is clean on the native and wasm32 targets.

## Known weaknesses and open questions

- **None of the feel tuning has been playtested:** grip, yaw response, recentre assist, drift slip angles, and check mass. All of it lives in `sim::car::TUNING`.
- **The attract-mode autopilot isn't perfect.** It may clip a rear corner about once per five minutes, and bumps into wrecks while creeping round them. Fine for attract mode, but visible.
- **Wrecks stay in the road** until the player passes them and they despawn. Pile-ups can leave debris across lanes. That's intended (Burnout-style), but it can feel unfair.
- **No car-to-car damage visuals** (no deformation).

## Revision after the first playtest (2026-11-24)

**User feedback** (touch on a phone):
- slow to turn in;
- keeps sliding sideways;
- bends push me wide;
- the camera no longer follows the car;
- the wheels don't look like they're spinning.

**Root cause:** steering asked for a constant *turn rate*. On touch, a finger held off-centre therefore kept turning the car until it slid across the lanes.

**Changes:**
- **Faster turn-in:** yaw response raised from 9/s to 18/s. The new test `turn_in_is_quick` checks that the car reaches at least 80% of its maximum yaw rate within 250 ms.
- **Self-centring scaled by (1 − |steer|), at 2.5/s.** Steering now sets a steady *heading*: a held input means "cross at this rate", and letting go straightens the car. Covered by `steering_sets_a_heading_and_letting_go_straightens_up`.
- **Road assist raised from 15% to 60%.** A hard bend at top speed now needs about a third of full steering, and running wide without steering is gentle.
- **Spin-outs:** the physical heading no longer keeps rotating during a stun (the visible spin is separate). The car recovers pointing nearly down the road instead of tracing a 16 m sideways arc.
- **Autopilot:** compensates for self-centring and looks further ahead. It drove five minutes in traffic with no spin-outs.
- **Chase camera:** it sits behind the car along 70% of the car's heading, so the car stays centred, and spin-outs don't drag it round.
- **Wheels:** now separate instances, each a unit mesh with a glowing six-spoke rim.
  - They roll with speed. The visible spin is capped at 30 rad/s to avoid the wagon-wheel strobe at 60 fps.
  - The player's front wheels steer.
  - Traffic wheels roll too, within 400 m of the camera.

The golden hash is now `0x5cb0a1880810eaeb`, and wasm matches it.
