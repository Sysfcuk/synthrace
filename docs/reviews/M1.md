# M1 review: "First Drive" (playable prototype)

**Status:** implemented, tested natively and in the browser, and deployed to https://sysfcuk.github.io/synthrace/.

**Not judged yet:** handling feel and the art direction. Both need the user's review.

## What was built

- **`sim` crate:** a deterministic game simulation with no platform dependencies.
  - **Seeded track generator.** A 30 km ring of 5 m segments, built from straights, sweepers, S-bends and rolling hills with eased entries and exits. The ring closes in height.
  - **Arcade handling model** (GAME_DESIGN §4):
    - acceleration curve, braking and coast drag;
    - speed-dependent steering authority, with digital steering ramping in over 120 ms and recentring over 80 ms;
    - centrifugal push proportional to curve × v²;
    - off-road deceleration of about 2 g down to a 38% speed cap, plus a steering penalty;
    - a verge limit that scrapes off speed;
    - visual yaw.
  - **Fixed 120 Hz step** with interpolated rendering, including across the seam where the ring wraps.
  - **PCG32 RNG** and an FNV state hash.
  - **Autopilot** for attract mode.
- **`procgen` crate:**
  - a flat-shaded low-poly sports car (about 300 triangles) with an HDR tail-light bar, neon shoulder strips, glowing wheel rims and a contact shadow;
  - a palm tree with a curved hexagonal trunk and nine drooping, V-folded fronds.
- **Renderer:**
  - an instanced, lit mesh pass that uses the same vertex-shader bend as the road, so the car and palms sit exactly on the curved, hilly world;
  - shading with violet and magenta ambient light, a low sunset key light, cyan and magenta neon rim light on silhouettes, a glossy sky reflection, and fog matched to the sky;
  - fog shared across all surfaces;
  - a chase camera that lags laterally, tilts with upcoming slopes, and widens the field of view with speed.
- **Visual drama:** the road's sideways swing is drawn at 1.8× and hills at 1.3×. The exaggeration is purely visual, because gameplay stays in track space.
- **Palm trees** (your request, brought forward from M4): seeded placement in clusters along both verges, roughly every 16–30 m, culled to the 1.25 km draw distance.
- **`game::scene`:** builds each frame from a simulation snapshot. The browser and the native screenshot tool share it, so screenshots match the game.
- **Browser:**
  - Attract mode lets the autopilot drive until you press a key or touch the screen.
  - Keyboard: arrow keys or WASD.
  - Touch: hold to accelerate, and finger x-position steers.
  - The debug overlay now shows speed in km/h and an off-road indicator.

## Known weaknesses

- **Handling feel is unvalidated by play.** The tuning values come from the design document and the tests, not from playtesting.
- **No collisions with palms.** You can drive through them; the verge limit is at 20 m. Scenery collisions are planned for M2.
- **Sun colour:** the sun still reads mostly yellow. Its magenta lower half is usually hidden behind the mountains or washed out by AgX tone mapping.
- **No in-world HUD yet.** The debug overlay stands in for it.
- **No gamepad support** (M2).
- **Long scenery triangles may not bend smoothly.** Palm geometry isn't tessellated along z. That's fine at palm scale, but needs care for larger props.
- **Car visual yaw** responds to lateral speed only. Drift angles arrive in M2.
- **Audio** is still the M0 spike loop, driven only by speed.

## Gate

The user needs to review these on the live build:

1. **Handling feel:** keyboard on desktop, touch on a phone.
2. **Art direction.**

Any tuning changes go into `sim::car::TUNING` and the scene constants. M2 "Speed and Feel" adds traffic, near-misses, drift, nitro, speed effects, gamepad support and engine audio.
